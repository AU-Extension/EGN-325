    How to use this interactive
    1. Start on the Timing ribbon tab. The clock (CLK) ticks in the top lane, your data signal D is below it, and the bottom two lanes show what a latch and an edge-triggered flip-flop store. A lit green pad or a green trace is a 1.
    2. Tap any D slot (on the board's logic analyzer or in the timing diagram) to draw your own signal, or load a preset such as a glitch while the clock is high.
    3. Press Run to sweep through time one slot at a time, or Step and Step back to move by hand. The caption explains what each storage element does at that moment, the clock rail shows the clock phase, and every rising clock edge flashes amber.
    4. Turn on Predict before you see it: at each rising edge (and when D changes while the latch is open) you answer what Q will be before it appears.
    5. Open the Register tab: set the D inputs and the load enable (LE), then press Clock tick and watch the bit cells latch.
    6. Open the Counter tab: tick the 4-bit counter from 0 to 15 and see which bits flip on each tick, then finish with the self-check.

    4-bit register with load enable

    A register is four D flip-flops that share one clock. Load enable (LE) decides whether a clock tick copies D into Q or keeps the old value.

    4-bit counter

    A counter is a register that loads its own value plus 1 on every tick. Four bits count from 0 to 15, then wrap back to 0.

    Things to notice

    • The latch copies D the whole time CLK is 1. The flip-flop looks at D only at a rising edge, using the value from just before it.
    • A glitch while CLK is 1 reaches the latch and stays there after the clock falls. The flip-flop never sees it.
    • With LE = 0 a clock tick changes nothing: the register keeps its value until a tick with LE = 1.
    • In the counter, bit 0 flips on every tick, and a higher bit flips only when every bit below it was 1.
    • After 15 (1111) the 4-bit counter wraps around to 0.
