Bay-4 computer simulator

Watch a whole computer run a program. Every instruction goes through the fetch-decode-execute cycle, and you can see each value travel on the buses, land in a register, and change the memory view in real time. This is the same Bay-4 CPU you trace in Week 5 and build in the final project.

How to use the simulator
  1. Press Step to move one micro-step: the caption explains what happens and the board animates it. Play runs the steps on their own, and the speed slider makes them faster or slower.
  2. Drag the board to orbit it, scroll or pinch to zoom, and click any chip (PC, ROM, IR, control unit, A, B, ALU, Z, OUT) to read what it does.
  3. Turn on Predict before execute. After each decode, the simulator asks what will change. Answer before you see it happen.
  4. Click the dot next to a ROM address to set a breakpoint. Play and Run to HLT stop when the PC reaches it.
  5. Load a preset or type your own program (one instruction per line, addresses optional), then compare the trace table with your hand trace.

Instruction memory (ROM)

16 words of 8 bits: the opcode (high 4 bits) and the operand (low 4 bits). The amber row is where the PC points; the blue row is the word being read right now.
