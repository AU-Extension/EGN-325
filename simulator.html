Things to notice

  • Every instruction starts the same way: the PC sends an address, the ROM sends back a word. Only the decode step tells instructions apart.
  • The top 4 bits of the word are the opcode and go to the control unit. The bottom 4 bits are the operand: a number for LDA and LDB, an address for JMP and JZ.
  • Only the ALU instructions (ADD, SUB, AND, OR) change the zero flag. JZ looks at the flag the last ALU instruction left behind.
  • The address bus has 4 lines because 4 bits name 16 ROM words. The instruction bus has 8 lines, one per bit of a word.
  • Arithmetic wraps around: 15 + 1 gives 0 and 0 − 1 gives 15, because the registers only have 4 bits.
