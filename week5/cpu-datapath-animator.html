Things to notice

  • Every instruction starts with the same fetch: the PC sends an address, the ROM sends back one 8-bit word.
  • Decode is where instructions differ. The opcode in the top 4 bits decides which control signals turn on.
  • Only ADD, SUB, AND, and OR update the zero flag, and JZ reads the flag the last ALU instruction left behind.
  • Registers have 4 bits, so 9 + 8 shows 1 and 0 - 1 shows 15. Watch the "Overflow: 9 + 8" sample.
How to use this interactive
  1. Pick a sample program, or write your own in the program box (one instruction per line) and press Load program.
  2. Press Step forward to move through fetch, decode, and execute. Watch the glowing packets travel on the buses: each bus has its own color, and the chips that take part in the phase light up. Step back undoes a phase.
  3. Read the narration and the control signals the decoder turns on for the current instruction.
  4. Rotate the board and click any chip to learn what it does, or switch to the datapath diagram. Use Next instruction, Play, and the speed slider to go faster.
  5. Turn on Predict before execute to guess each result before you see it, then try the self-check below.
